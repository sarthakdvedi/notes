
## I. do / does / did-

MY UNDERSTANDING ( crux )
```
if(singular nouns / 3rd person) "does"  // only 1 case for does
else "do"
```

### 1. When to use **"Do"**

Use **do** for:

- **1st person:** _I, we_ ("Do I know you?", "Do we need tickets?")
    
- **2nd person:** _you_ ("Do you need help?")
    
- **Plural 3rd person / Plural nouns:** _they, the cars, my friends_ ("Do they work here?", "Do the machines run?")
    

### 2. When to use **"Does"**

Use **does** for:

- **Singular 3rd person / Singular nouns:** _he, she, it, the car, my friend_ ("Does he know?", "Does it work?", "Does the phone ring?")

eg :
- **Singular 3rd person:** "Does **he** work?" / "Does **the machine** work?"
    
- **Plural 3rd person:** "Do **they** work?" / "Do **the machines** work?"

### 3. Did (The Past Blueprint)

Use **did** for actions that happened and **completely finished** in the past.

- **When to use it:** Past tense.
- **The Golden Rule:** When you use _did_ or _did not_, the action word that follows must go back to its base form (present form). You never use two past tense words together.
    - ❌ _Incorrect:_ "I **did not received** the mail." (Two past tenses).
    - _Correct:_ "I **did not receive** the mail." (_Did_ already shows it's the past).
    - _Correct Question:_ "**Did** you **call** him?" (Not _"Did you called"_).

## II. have / has / had -

### 1. Have / Has (The Bridge to Now)

Use **have** (or **has**) when an action started in the past, but it is **still important right now** or is **not finished**. This is why we use it for a missing email.

- **When to use it:** Connecting the past to the present.
- **The Golden Rule:** Always pair _have_ with the past form of the action word (called the past participle, like _received_, _seen_, or _gone_).
    - _Correct:_ "I **have not received** the mail yet." (Meaning: It didn't arrive in the past, and I am still waiting for it _right now_).
    - _Correct:_ "I **have lost** my keys." (They were lost in the past, and I still don't have them _now_).

### 2. Had (The Past Before the Past)

Use **had** when you are talking about **two things that happened in the past**, and you want to show which one happened _first_.

- **When to use it:** The furthest past.
- **Example:** "When I got to the station, the train **had** already **left**."
    - Both events are in the past. But first, the train left. Then, I arrived.
